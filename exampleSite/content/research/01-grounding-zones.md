Grounding zones mark the transition from a glacier resting on rock to floating in the ocean. They act as gates on ice flux, controlling how much ice contributes to sea-level rise. Tides, subglacial meltwater outflow, bed composition, and long-term change all shape how the ocean reaches into this region.
